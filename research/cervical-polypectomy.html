Cervical Polypectomy During Pregnancy

Comparison of Surgical and Obstetric Outcomes Between Two Types of Cervical Polypectomy During Pregnancy

Ongoing real-world clinical-outcomes research comparing traditional and vaginoscopy polypectomy during pregnancy.
Ongoing real-world studyENAR 2025 poster

Overview

This ongoing retrospective clinical-outcomes study compares traditional polypectomy with vaginoscopy polypectomy during pregnancy, considering both procedural outcomes and time to preterm birth.

Analytical problem

The study asked whether outcomes differed by surgical approach, whether effects varied between cervical and decidual polyps, and which demographic, clinical, or postoperative factors were associated with preterm birth.

Why it matters

Traditional forceps-based polypectomy can involve bleeding and discomfort during pregnancy. Vaginoscopy polypectomy is a less-invasive alternative, but its safety and effectiveness relative to traditional surgery—across cervical and decidual polyps—require careful evaluation. The study therefore considers procedural burden and obstetric follow-up together.

My contribution

I serve as the lead statistician: I designed the statistical analysis plan, collaborate with clinicians at Jiangxi Maternal & Child Health Hospital who collect the confidential real-world data, and conduct the primary statistical analyses. I was also the presenting author of the ENAR 2025 poster.

Data and study design

The study uses confidential real-world data from pregnant patients treated at Jiangxi Maternal & Child Health Hospital. The analysis includes baseline characteristics, surgical measures, postoperative complications, and obstetric follow-up. Participant-level data are not distributed through this site.

Statistical methods

  • Descriptive comparisons using chi-square, t-test, or Wilcoxon procedures as appropriate
  • Kaplan–Meier curves for gestational age at delivery
  • Cox proportional hazards models for preterm birth risk
  • Adjusted modeling using clinically relevant covariates and backward variable selection

Results and practical contribution

The ENAR poster reported no significant difference in the total count of adverse events between surgical groups. Vaginoscopy polypectomy was associated with shorter procedure time and lower bleeding in selected comparisons, while recurrent bleeding and high white-blood-cell count were associated with preterm-birth risk. These interim observational findings motivate continued analysis; they should not be interpreted as randomized comparative-effectiveness or formal noninferiority evidence.

Limitations

The study was retrospective and relatively small. Potential confounding, subgroup instability, selection into treatment, and the censoring assumptions used for time-to-event analysis limit causal and clinical interpretation.

Reproducibility and availability

Only the approved conference poster is public. The real-world participant-level data and analysis dataset remain confidential.
